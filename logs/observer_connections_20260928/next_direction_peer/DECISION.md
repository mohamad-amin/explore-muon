# Direction choice after closing early mixed nonlinearity

2026-09-28. Both independent discussions identify a distinct plausible
connection, and neither recommends reopening the failed mixed-response test.

The first bounded follow-up is the exact saved-weight angular/radial
comparison in `../angular_clock/PROTOCOL.md`. It targets a specific missing
premise in an already completed controlled LR×momentum contrast. A preliminary
2×-horizon screen suggests different Q/K radii and turns at equal nominal
step norms. The fixed twelve-state factorial can now determine whether LR
changes are substantially absorbed by radius adaptation. It measures actual
writes and retains all heads rather than doing a general norm inventory.

The second review identifies a different functional observation: retained
loss minus entropy gives the exact derivative of loss under global logit
scaling. Its large phase/state variation motivates a later cross-fitted
confidence-versus-shape discriminator at the six completed schedule endpoints.
This proposal is retained, not scored or merged with the angular criterion.
No predicted causal link between the two observations is assumed. The angular
test's result will not authorize an optimizer intervention; neither would an
output calibration result.

This ordering adds a missing physical quantity to an existing factorial
without new model calls. It does not substitute norm correlations for the
proposed direct predictive discriminator or declare the broad goal complete.
No train/GPU job, scalar-temperature fit or normalization change is launched
by this decision.

Known precedents constrain novelty and transfer:

- [Arora, Li and Lyu, auto rate-tuning by batch normalization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03981)
  studies scale-invariance-induced rate adaptation. Its SGD/BN theory does
  not directly establish the behavior of finite-NS SOAP-PD with QK RMSNorm.
- [Li, Lyu and Arora, intrinsic learning rate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.02916)
  connects normalization, weight decay and effective rates. This motivates
  distinguishing geometry from nominal LR; it is not proof of the new clock.
- [Guo et al., calibration of modern neural networks](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html)
  establishes temperature scaling as a calibration tool. The proposed use
  here is a diagnostic of a particular saved schedule comparison, not a
  new calibration method.
