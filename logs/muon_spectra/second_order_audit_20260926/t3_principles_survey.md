# Track 3 leaderboard: principle behind each method

Source commit `bc3a0c2d`. Every row below was checked against the primary source listed (log source code, PR body via GitHub API, result README, or the cited paper's abstract) unless marked **unverified**. "Verified" means I read the actual optimizer code or an author-written primary text, not just the leaderboard's one-line gloss.

**Legend.** `NS` = Newton–Schulz orthogonalization / `msign(X)=UV^T` for `X=UΣV^T`. `M` = momentum-blended gradient. `G` = raw minibatch gradient. `W` = weight matrix, `W₀` = its init. `σ` = singular value. `L=EMA(GG^T)`, `R=EMA(G^TG)` = Shampoo/SOAP row/col Gram statistics (a curvature **proxy**, not activations or Hessian-vector products). "hyperball" = `scale_invariant_update_`: take the preconditioned step, then rescale back onto the parameter's original Frobenius sphere — used instead of weight decay. Roles are from {R=basis/rotation, M=per-direction magnitude, S=scale/hyperball/decay, N=noise-averaging-in-time, X=cross-layer coupling}, primary listed first.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **Statistics gap vs. the framework's premise:** almost every "curvature-aware" method here substitutes gradient/momentum Gram matrices (`GG^T`,`G^TG`) for real curvature; only Newton-Muon uses actual activations, and **zero** surveyed methods use Hessian-vector products, despite "Newton"/"curvature-informed" branding on several (Newton-Muon, PSGD's cited lineage).
2. Three mathematically distinct things get called "preconditioning": (a) **change-of-basis** before flattening (SOAP-Muon, PMuon, Newton-Muon, PSGD, one-sided Shampoo — genuine R), (b) **elementwise/diagonal-in-standard-basis** rescaling before NS (Muon² — M dressed as conditioning), (c) **post-hoc reshaping of a fixed spectrum** with no new basis at all (Contra-/Soft-/Dyn-Muon, Tempered-Polar, Aurora, NorMuon — pure M).
3. **S is the most heavily reused role** (~10/23 entries: hyperball family, u/w-floor, RowFloor, radial brake, SODA-anchor, CWD, Muown, PSGD's hyperball swap, SOAP-H/KL-SOAP) — in this fixed-step-budget regime, controlling the update-to-weight norm ratio is the most heavily mined lever, more than exotic R or M changes.
4. **N is near-universal as plumbing but rarely the headline claim** — genuine N-primary novelty appears only in MuLoCo (multi-step outer loop) and the Tail-EMA readout bundled into #46 (eval-time-only blend); both act at an unusual timescale, not per-step momentum.
5. **X is the rarest role** — Circuit-Muon is the only entry requiring more than one weight tensor's own statistics, and the newest idea in the lineage (2026/05/23).
6. Several "schedules" (DynMuon's `p_t`, Tempered-Polar's ρ-ramp, Contra→Soft blend weight, u/w-floor and radial-brake late retunes) are deterministic functions of step count layered on an R/M/S mechanism — classified here by the underlying mechanism, not counted as N, since N means noise-averaging, not "changes over training time."
7. A four-way family (Contra-Muon, Soft-Muon, DynMuon, Tempered-Polar) all soften Muon's `f(σ)=1` back toward the raw spectrum via different mechanics (linear subtraction, truncated-NS convex combination, scheduled power, clipped residual-blend); DynMuon's paper gives this family its clearest theoretical grounding (curvature/noise/SNR-dependent choice of exponent).
8. Two verified citation/compliance problems: #13's MuLoCo link (arXiv 2502.07314) resolves to an unrelated automata-theory paper (correct: 2505.23725); #35's log bare-imports Meta's `distributed_shampoo` package, apparently violating the benchmark's own reproducibility rule.
9. The benchmark's own predeclared pairwise test shows three "new records" (#11 vs #9, #13 vs #11/#8, #16 vs #14) are **not** statistically distinguishable from their immediate predecessor, despite each clearing the global 3.28 bar — the step-count column is not a clean ranking.
10. #16's oddly-named log (`fake_log_from_seed0.txt`) was checked directly against the PR's reported seed-0 numbers at 5 checkpoints and matches exactly — not fabricated, just an unfortunate filename.
11. The whole hyperball/u-w-floor/RowFloor/radial-brake/SODA/CWD/Muown cluster is one idea at increasing granularity (whole-tensor → per-row → per-coordinate) with different gates (norm ratio, radial sign, coordinate sign, distance-from-init).
12. Only Newton-Muon and PMuon isolate a single mechanism against an unchanged baseline with zero retuning — the cleanest causal attributions here; most other entries stack 3–6 simultaneous changes, so component-level credit beyond the authors' own ablations is not independently verifiable from the leaderboard alone.
