### Tiny surrogate qualification: optimizer geometry, batch and momentum (2026-09-28 13:55 CDT)

**User direction.** Establish a small, standalone tiny-GPT-like setup before further full-pipeline work. Use only cluster GPUs with strictly less than 48 GB VRAM. Target the performance ordering Adam < Muon < PD-Muon < TS-Muon < SOAP-Muon in PD coordinates, increasing geometry benefit with batch size, and shorter useful momentum at larger batch. The eventual motivation is substantial simplification/memory or convergence improvements, including SOAP in TS coordinates; those are not substitutes for qualifying the surrogate first. Existing large-model and observer work is separate and is neither launched nor modified by this branch.

**Missing premise.** The existing effects were measured in a roughly 100M transformer with a large vocabulary on FineWeb. A tiny character model has different data repetition, gradient noise and a much smaller auxiliary head. Its ranking and dynamics cannot be assumed to transfer. TS was stronger than PD at 4M in the reference but approximately tied at 16M; the complete chain is sought in at least one informative batch regime, while batch and momentum effects must also reproduce.

**Mechanism and competitor.** Input/output anisotropy, gradient-coordinate normalization and the noise/lag/stability tradeoff could survive small width and vocabulary. Alternatively, corpus overfitting, auxiliary-head dominance in the original, or a short step horizon may prevent a faithful surrogate. A favorable single endpoint, untuned Adam/Muon, or a large-batch momentum gain caused only by too few steps does not establish transfer.

**Independent peer discussion.** A fresh peer supported a staged screen, keeping all three requirements separate. It flagged validation saturation, covariance clocks, the changed body/head balance, and fixed-token versus fixed-step confounding. A separate code audit established the existing optimizer API and the distinction between predictive-label GN output statistics and data-label empirical Fisher statistics. Their guidance is implemented in the following frozen design.

### Literature check: a uniform two-pole control for the split (2026-09-30 16:08 CDT)

**Papers (the user pointed to them).**
- AdEMAMix (arXiv 2409.03137): Adam with a fast and a very slow EMA of the gradient, mixed non-convexly (β1 0.9, β3 0.9999, α 4–10, warmup schedulers for α and β3), the same kernel in every coordinate.
- EMA-Nesterov (2605.25395): the gradient is taken at a lookahead point along an EMA of parameter updates (γ 0.99–0.995), because the one-step direction extrapolates the across-valley oscillation. The lookahead is off in warmup and in the decay.
- Bi-Maxwell (2608.22994): Muon with a convex two-pole memory (β 0.85 and 0.98, w 0.44, switched on at about 35% of training), 40–55 steps earlier to target on modded-nanogpt. A read-only probe finds that the preferred memory differs by more than 10× between momentum-strength bands and moves with the LR: middle and weak bands shorten at a high constant LR; every band lengthens as the LR decays, the weak bands most.

**Relation.** All three change the temporal kernel and apply it identically in every direction; none assigns β by direction. Bi-Maxwell's measurement is closest to ours: direction-dependent optimal memory that lengthens as the LR decays, as in our 262K cooldown. It argues that a uniform two-pole kernel serves different modes through their frequency content (a flipping mode is carried by the fast branch). That is an alternative explanation of the split: the gain could come from the kernel's shape rather than from assigning β by input variance. Its tracking proxy (random-walk drift plus white noise) has no oscillation term: a flipping band registers as fast drift and is assigned a short memory. At 1M that is the reverse split, which lost.

**Control.** At 1M every input-variance bin oscillates, so a uniform kernel cannot damp the high-variance band's flip more than the rest's; the split can. Prediction: uniform two-pole kernels land near β 0.8, short of the split. Runs at 1M (92 steps, aux ×1, 1 GPU), with runner `split_parallel_v3.py` (v2 plus a uniform "twopole" rule; a CPU smoke with both poles at 0.9 reproduces the control to 1e-8 in validation NLL):
- two-pole (0.5, 0.9) with w 0.5 and w 0.25 from step 2 (period-2 gains 0.19 and 0.12 of the persistent gain; β 0.8: 0.11, β 0.9: 0.05);
- Bi-Maxwell's recipe as published (0.85, 0.98, w 0.4385, switched on at 35% of the steps).

Readout against p1_pd1m_auxx1_{control, beta08, split05}: step-equivalent ratio. If a uniform kernel matches the split, the spatial assignment is not needed. If none comes close, it is.
